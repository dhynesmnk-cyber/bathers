The building at 1 Mineral Springs Reserve Road has been drawing people to the waters since 1895. Hepburn Springs sits over one of the most concentrated natural mineral spring systems in Australia, and the bathhouse was established specifically to make use of them. The mineral water feeding the pools comes directly from those springs rather than being treated and reconstituted elsewhere.

The bathing areas include a mineral-rich Salt Pool and the Sanctuary Mineral Bathing zone. The word "mineral" carries real weight here — the source is a documented natural spring, not a marketing category. No temperatures are published for the pools in available records.

The thermal sequence on offer is framed as a contrast therapy ritual: heat from the dry sauna, followed by cold via an overhead cascade bucket that releases mineral water, followed by immersion in the mineral baths. It is European bathhouse logic applied to an Australian spring, and the site has had 130 years to refine the arrangement. The cold component is worth noting precisely: it is a cascade rather than a dedicated plunge pool, which is a different experience from full submersion.

The Hepburn Pavilion Café operates separately from the bathing schedule — Monday to Friday 8:30 AM to 4:00 PM, Saturday to 5:00 PM, Sunday to 4:00 PM — so food is available before most sessions without needing to leave the reserve.
