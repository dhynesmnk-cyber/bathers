In a small town near the Queensland border, where the flat country stretches out and the road through has little to slow you down, the Boomi Artesian Bath is the kind of place that earns a detour. The water comes up from 1,222 metres below ground — deep enough to be genuinely hot when it arrives — and has been doing so, in one form or another, since the first pool was built here in 1905. The present complex dates from 1975, with a spa pool addition in 1996.

There are three pools. The hot artesian spa pool is the centrepiece, fed by that deep water and fitted with jet spas. The 25-metre pool runs cooler — visitors describe it as mildly heated — and works well as a lap pool or a counterpoint after time in the spa. Between them, a wading pool sits at an intermediate temperature, which makes it useful if you're travelling with children or just want somewhere to ease in. Google reviews consistently mention how the water feels on joints, and the rating sits at 4.5 from 68 reviews — a solid signal for a small regional facility.

The setting does its part too. The complex is described as quiet and well-shaded, with a lush green lawn under shade sails, poolside lounging, and an adjacent Community Co-op that covers the practical bases: groceries, a cafe, a free BBQ, a playground, and tennis courts. There's also a caravan park right next door — $25 a night with power and water — which explains why some visitors who intended to stop briefly end up staying three nights.

Entry is $6 for adults, $5 for pensioners, and re-entry is permitted, so there's no pressure to clock your session. For visitors with limited mobility, the 25-metre pool has easy-access steps, and the facility has a portable hoist and sling as well as a bariatric wheelchair available.

The baths open daily in two sessions — 9 AM to noon, and 2 PM to 5 PM. It's worth noting that hours like these can shift seasonally at community-run facilities; if you're making a specific trip, a call to 02 6753 5336 beforehand is worth the two minutes.
