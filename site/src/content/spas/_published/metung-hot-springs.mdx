Metung Hot Springs draws its water from 500 metres underground. At the source it comes up at 45 degrees, carrying natural minerals and trace elements from the geothermal aquifer below East Gippsland — and the setting above ground matches that slow-earth drama: the property overlooks the Gippsland Lakes, one of the largest inland waterways in the southern hemisphere.

<Pull>The water rises from 500 metres underground at 45 degrees — and the three outdoor precincts are arranged across the property to make the most of the lake views above it.</Pull>

The bathing area is organised into three precincts: Bathing Ridge, the Lagoon, and the Hilltop Escarpment. Guests report the pools are naturally warm, mostly shaded, and that arriving early — especially at sunrise — is worth the early alarm. Sauna rooms are on site; their type isn't specified in the available record.

The private soaking barrels are the detail most people mention. They sit on the decks of the glamping tents and fill with either geothermal water or hot tap water — the geothermal supply in the barrels runs cooler than the main pools, but the privacy trade-off is obvious. The glamping tents themselves are safari-style, with ensuite bathrooms, king-size four-poster beds, and private decks. Hillside cabins with views over the lagoon and lake are also available.

Treatments on offer include Larn'wa Aboriginal Lore wellness rituals and Synthesis Organics native botanical spa treatments. A cafe on site serves light refreshments and drinks between soaks.

<Pull>The soaking barrels on the glamping tent decks are what most people end up talking about — geothermal water, lake views, and no one else around.</Pull>

With a 4.4 rating across 746 Google reviews, it reads as a place that consistently delivers on what it's selling: genuinely warm geothermal water in a setting that earns the drive from Melbourne.

Change rooms are on site, but hair dryers aren't provided — bring your own if wet hair in cold weather is going to be a problem. The springs are open daily from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM. At least one day visitor reports paying $40 for entry. Getting there without a car takes some planning: V/Line to Bairnsdale, then a coach to Swan Reach, then Lakes Taxi Service for the last leg — roughly 9 minutes and around $45–$50 one way. Worth factoring in before you go.
