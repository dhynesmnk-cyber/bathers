Out in the flat country south of Walgett, about 100 metres off the Kamilaroi Highway on a sealed road, an artesian bore pushes water to the surface at a steady 36°C — and has been doing so long enough that a small campground has grown up around it. The bath is the point. Everything else — the powered sites, the showers, the BBQs, the dump point — is infrastructure that makes it practical to stay a few nights rather than drive in and out.

<Pull>An artesian bore pushes water to the surface at a steady 36°C, and has been doing so long enough that a whole campground has grown up around it.</Pull>

The water is soft and warm rather than hot. At 36°C it sits close to body temperature, which means you can stay in without the clock running against you the way it does in a hotter spa. There's no cold plunge documented here, no sauna circuit — just the one bath and the flat outback sky above it. The artesian water has a reputation for easing aching muscles.

Practically speaking, it runs 24 hours on most days. Wednesday has a gap: it closes at 9:00 AM and reopens at 1:00 PM. Camping costs $5–$10 per night based on visitor accounts, on a donation basis, and powered sites are available. The nearest food or supplies are 3 km away in Burren Junction proper, so stock up before you arrive.

One practical note worth flagging: the baths were closed for works and were due to reopen in April 2026. The current status isn't certain from the published record, so a call to (02) 6828 6133 before making the trip out is sensible — it's a long way to drive to a locked gate. The venue also doesn't cater for people with access needs, which is stated directly in the site information.

With 212 Google reviews averaging 4.1, it reads as a place that delivers on what it is — a free or near-free bush soak with basic but functional facilities. No booking required; just show up.
