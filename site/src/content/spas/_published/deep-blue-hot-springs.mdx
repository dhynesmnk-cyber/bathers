On Worm Bay Road at the western edge of Warrnambool, geothermal mineral water surfaces into a series of open-air rock pools set against the sea. The water is drawn from deep underground and is mineral-rich, and the setting is genuinely coastal rather than the kind of landscaped inland park that usually comes to mind.

The pool circuit includes cascading thermal waterfalls and sensory caves alongside the main rock pools. No cold plunge is listed among the facilities, and no specific water temperatures are documented.

A cold evening makes the warm water feel noticeably better — twilight sessions get called out specifically for that reason.

At 1,497 Google ratings averaging 4.4, the venue has a solid and well-tested reputation. Consistent positives in visitor accounts are the cleanliness and maintenance of the pools, the cave-like atmosphere, and the experience of soaking outdoors in cold air. The twilight session in particular draws strong praise — the contrast between a 10-degree evening and warm mineral water is, by multiple accounts, the point.

A handful of criticisms show up repeatedly and are worth knowing about: the site can feel crowded during popular sessions, visible cameras and hotel rooms overlooking parts of the facility affect the sense of privacy for some visitors, and the landscaping includes a mix of natural and artificial rock features. The changeroom facilities — with private cubicles and individual showers — come off consistently well. One note on lockers: they're not included in the standard entry price, with at least one hotel guest flagging an $8 charge as an unexpected addition.

The venue runs sessions throughout the day and into the evening, opening at 7:00 AM and closing at 9:30 PM every day of the week. The hot springs sit within a larger property that also houses a hotel, so some sessions may include guests from the accommodation side — midweek visits tend to be quieter. Booking ahead is sensible, particularly for twilight slots.
