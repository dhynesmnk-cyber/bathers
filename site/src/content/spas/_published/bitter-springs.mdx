Bitter Springs sits inside Elsey National Park, about 2 kilometres from the small town of Mataranka in the Katherine region of the Northern Territory. It's a natural thermal spring, not a built facility. The pools are fed directly from underground, surrounded by palms and tropical woodland, and access from the car park is along a short paved path with stairs and handrails.

The water temperature isn't published anywhere. What is clear is that these are genuine geothermal springs with warm water, popular with swimmers doing the loop from one end to the other and floating back on the current. The 500-metre loop walk nearby connects the two ends of the swimming stretch.

Entry runs through the NT Parks Visitor Pass system, which is straightforward. A single-day adult pass is $10; if you're planning to stay in the region for a while, the two-week pass at $30 is reasonable value. NT residents don't need a pass at all. Children aged 5 to 17 pay half price, and a family pass covering two adults and up to four children is $25 for a day.

The one hard constraint is seasonal. Bitter Springs closes every November and doesn't reopen until May or June, when the wet-season flooding has subsided. Plan around that if you're coming from interstate. The dry-season window is long enough to work with, but it's worth knowing before you build a trip around it.

Facilities on site are basic: car park, picnic area, public toilets. There's no café, no towel hire, no lockers. Bring everything you need. The wheelchair path to the water is manageable, though getting in and out of the water itself requires someone to help. Worth knowing ahead of time if that applies to your group.

Birdwatching is listed as one of the site's activities, which tells you something about the pace of the place. This is the Northern Territory's national park system, not a spa circuit. Come for the spring water and the palms and the particular quiet of a thermal pool that's been here rather longer than any built facility.
