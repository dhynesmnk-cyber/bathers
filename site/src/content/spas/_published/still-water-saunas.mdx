On Lake Sambell in Beechworth, a floating sauna sits at the edge of the water in one of Victoria's better-preserved Gold Rush towns. The High Country surrounds it.

The sauna itself is the product of considered Scandinavian craft: double-layered softwood construction, a stone stove, and a löyly approach that involves eucalyptus oil worked into the water before it hits the rocks. The stone stove, the eucalyptus oil, the lake outside – the whole thing hangs together in a way that's hard to fake.

There's no dedicated cold plunge pool. Instead, you go into Lake Sambell, which is the point. Natural water, whatever temperature the season gives you. Reviewers consistently flag the lake dip as the highlight – the contrast between a well-heated sauna and a jump into open water has its own logic that a fibreglass tub can't quite replicate.

The sauna runs at a human scale. A Shared Pass fits up to five people including yourself; a Private Pass means that same limit is your group only. Based on guest reports, sessions run roughly an hour, though the venue doesn't publish session lengths officially. The owner, who goes by Mick in at least one review, is clearly hands-on. One visitor noted him wiping down the seats and the full glass panel between every session.

The changerooms are well-fitted, which matters when you're wet and cold and need a moment to come back to yourself.

Forty-seven Google reviews averaging a perfect five stars is unusual for anything, let alone a floating sauna on a regional lake. The sentiment is consistent: people rate the craft of the build, the efficiency of the heat, and the quality of the lake plunge. A couple of visitors mentioned they'd have liked it hotter, which is worth knowing if you run on the higher end of sauna preference.

Beechworth is around two and a half hours from Melbourne, so this is a destination visit. The venue is on McConville Avenue beside the lake. Hours run Monday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday from 8am to 6pm, and Wednesday to Thursday from 6pm to 9pm. Tuesday is closed. Pricing amounts aren't listed publicly, but the pass structure (shared or private, with multi-pass options) gives you a sense of how sessions are sold. If you're going as a group and want the place to yourselves, the Private Pass is the way to go.
