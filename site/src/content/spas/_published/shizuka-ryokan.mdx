On Lakeside Drive in Hepburn, about ninety minutes out of Melbourne, Shizuka Ryokan sits on four acres of Victorian bushland and operates according to a Japanese inn tradition that predates modern spa culture by several centuries. The name comes from a concept of hospitality — *omotenashi*, often translated as hospitality from the heart — and the design follows through: tatami mats, shoji screens, futon bedding, and an architecture that takes the ryokan model seriously rather than gesturing at it.

The property carries the full name "Japanese Wellness Retreat & Bathhouse," and guest accounts confirm that mineral bathing is part of the offering. What the documented record doesn't supply are the specifics: pool temperatures, whether bathing is private or shared, what the full treatment menu looks like, or how day visitors access the bathhouse relative to overnight guests. The honest answer is that the venue warrants a phone call — (03) 5348 2030 — before you turn up expecting a specific circuit.

What is clear is that the experience is built around accommodation first. There are six guestrooms. There's a restaurant on-site that gets good marks for breakfast in particular. The property is inspired, according to its own history, by the ryokans that sheltered samurai travelling between Japan's natural springs — which at least sets the ambition at something more considered than a spa weekend.

Shizuka holds a 4.8 rating across 194 Google reviews. The reviews lean heavily on the sense of being transported: people mention the Japanese atmosphere and massage therapists by name. A dissenting voice in the mix flags dated rooms and thin walls, which is worth knowing if privacy and quiet matter to you — a property with six rooms sharing walls can only do so much.

Hours vary by day: the venue runs until 9pm on Mondays, Thursdays, Fridays, and Saturdays; until 8pm on Sundays; and closes at 5pm on Tuesdays and Wednesdays. If you're coming from Melbourne for a day visit rather than a stay, the longer evening sessions give you more to work with. Book treatments in advance — a place running six rooms and a small treatment roster won't hold space for long.
