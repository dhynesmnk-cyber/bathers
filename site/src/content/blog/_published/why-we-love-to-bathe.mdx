## An old habit

People have been bathing together for a very long time. Roman thermae, Turkish hammams, Finnish saunas, Japanese onsen, the mineral springs that towns grew up around. Long before anyone called it wellness, they understood something we keep rediscovering: heat, cold and water do good things to a body and a mind.

We love it for that lineage, and for the plain fact of how it feels.

## The pleasures of it

There is the first heat of a sauna, when your shoulders finally drop. There is the shock of a cold plunge, brief and total, that leaves you clear-headed and oddly cheerful. There is the slow float in a warm magnesium pool, where time stops mattering. None of it requires you to be good at anything. You just show up and let water and temperature do the work.

It is also, quietly, social. A bathhouse is one of the few places left where people sit near one another without screens, without a transaction in progress, without needing to perform. You share the heat and mostly leave each other alone, which is its own kind of company.

## Worth protecting

We built this directory because we think these places are worth finding and worth keeping. A good bathhouse is a small piece of public calm. The least we can do is make it easy to find one.
